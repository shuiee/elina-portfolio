<Section id="question" label="The question">

<p class="lede">{frontmatter.question}</p>

A handball venue for Birmingham 2022 that had to be iconic for two weeks and
demountable afterwards. Temporary is the difficult part: the building has to be worth
looking at, and it has to come apart, with the dismantling considered from the start
rather than added at the end of the drawings.

Two schemes came out of the concept phase, and they were good at different things.
That is the point at which a team can end up choosing by preference rather than by
evidence.

  rejected="Choosing the more iconic one"
>
Scheme 1 looked better. Without criteria written down first, that impression would
have carried the appraisal, and the reasons would have been assembled afterwards.

Fixing the five terms in advance made it possible to say precisely where each scheme
won, and that is what allowed the team to stop treating it as a choice between two.
</CriterionBlock>

<Step n="02" title="Detailed structure">
Thirteen modular three-pinned glulam arches spanning 60 m, with diagonal bracing.
A recycled ETFE roof membrane, cross-laminated timber floors and walls, and
galvanised steel connections — chosen so that the demounting sequence has somewhere
to go: bolted steel connections come apart, cast ones do not.

<Figure ratio="16/9" caption="TODO(elina): arch elevation with the polygon façade pattern applied, and the ETFE roof unit detail." />
</Step>

<Step n="03" title="Construction sequence, planned by hand">
The erection sequence was worked through step by step rather than assumed, against
four things that decide whether a temporary building is actually buildable: erection
stability at every intermediate stage, working-at-height exposure, connection
tolerances, and weather dependency.

A structure that is only stable once complete is a different and more hazardous
proposition than one that is stable at every stage of erection.
</Step>

<Section id="showed" label="What it showed">

<div class="prose">

The appraisal did not pick a winner. Once the five criteria were applied separately,
it was clear the schemes were not competing on the same axis. Scheme 1 won on
structure, constructability and programme. Scheme 2 won on the façade, which was an
aesthetic judgement and was allowed to be one, because the criteria made clear which
judgement was being made on what grounds.

The final building is a Scheme 1 structure with a Scheme 2 façade — a reasoned
outcome rather than a compromise.

</div>

</Section>

<Section id="means" label="What it means">

<div class="prose">

Two schemes against five criteria is not a choice between two options. It is ten
findings, and the resolution often sits between them.

The construction sequencing makes the same point. Designing how a temporary building
comes apart is not a downstream task: it determines the connection types, which
determine the material palette, which determine what the façade can be.

**Status:** academic concept design. Nothing was built.

</div>
