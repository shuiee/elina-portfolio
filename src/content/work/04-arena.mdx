<Section id="question" label="The question">

<p class="lede">{frontmatter.question}</p>

A handball venue for Birmingham 2022 that had to be iconic for two weeks and
demountable afterwards. Temporary is the hard part: the building has to be worth
looking at, and it has to come apart without the taking-apart being an afterthought
bolted on at the end of the drawings.

Two schemes came out of the concept phase, and they were good at different things.
That is the situation where a team quietly picks whichever one the strongest opinion
in the room prefers.

<CriterionBlock

    ["Graded on", "structural clarity · construction practicality · material efficiency · environmental performance · programme and cost impact"],
    ["Applied to", "Both schemes, independently, before any decision was taken"],
  ]}
  rejected="Choosing the more iconic one"
>
Scheme 1 looked better. With no criteria written down first, that would have been
the whole appraisal, and the reasons would have been reverse-engineered afterwards.

Fixing the five terms in advance made it possible to say precisely where each scheme
won — and that turned out to be the thing that let the team stop treating it as a
choice between two.
</CriterionBlock>

<Step n="02" title="Detailed structure">
Thirteen modular three-pinned glulam arches spanning 60 m, with diagonal bracing.
A recycled ETFE roof membrane, cross-laminated timber floors and walls, and
galvanised steel connections — chosen so that the demounting sequence has somewhere
to go: bolted steel connections come apart, cast ones do not.

<Figure ratio="16/9" caption="TODO(elina): arch elevation with the polygon façade pattern applied, and the ETFE roof unit detail." />
</Step>

<Step n="03" title="Construction sequence, planned by hand">
The erection sequence was worked through step by step rather than assumed, against
four things that decide whether a temporary building is actually buildable: erection
stability at every intermediate stage, working-at-height exposure, connection
tolerances, and weather dependency.

A structure that is only stable once complete is a different and more dangerous
building than one that is stable at each stage of going up.
</Step>

<Section id="showed" label="What it showed">

<div class="prose">

The appraisal did not pick a winner, because once the five criteria were applied
separately it was clear the schemes were not competing on the same axis. Scheme 1 won
on structure, constructability and programme. Scheme 2 won on the façade — which was
an aesthetic judgement, and was allowed to be one, because the criteria made it
explicit which judgement was being made on what grounds.

The final building is a Scheme 1 structure wearing a Scheme 2 skin, and that was a
defensible outcome rather than a compromise.

</div>

</Section>

<Section id="means" label="What it means">

<div class="prose">

Two schemes with five criteria is not a choice between two schemes. It is ten
findings, and the resolution is usually in the seam.

The construction sequencing is the other half of the same argument. Designing how a
temporary building comes apart is not a downstream task — it decides the connection
types, which decides the material palette, which decides what the façade can be.

**Status:** academic concept design. Nothing was built.

</div>
