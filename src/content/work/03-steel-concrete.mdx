<Section id="question" label="The question">

<p class="lede">{frontmatter.question}</p>

Steel–concrete (SC) systems are modular. They shorten programme, cut labour at
height, and improve through-thickness resilience — all of which matter in nuclear
construction, where reinforced concrete's rebar assembly and curing times create
bottlenecks inside a heavily regulated schedule.

None of that is worth anything unless the behaviour can be shown to be equivalent
under the codes. So the question is narrow on purpose: one 10 × 10 m slab in a
two-storey reference building, and whether the SC version does the same structural
job as the RC version it would replace.

<Section id="criteria" label="What counts as good">

<CriterionBlock
  claim="A difference only counts if it survives the model being slightly wrong."
  rows={[
    ["Instrument", "±10% sensitivity check on the directional stiffness modifiers"],
    ["Graded on", "Whether the RC-to-SC difference persists when the modifiers move, or collapses with them"],
    ["Verified against", "Hand calculations for bending and shear to Eurocode 2, before any comparison was made"],
  ]}
  figures={[
    ["±10%", "applied to the stiffness modifiers"],
  ]}
  rejected="Taking the model output as the finding"
>
The SC slab is represented as an equivalent orthotropic slab: uniform thickness and
mass, with directional stiffness modifiers standing in for the real behaviour of the
system. Those modifiers are an assumption I made.

If the RC-to-SC difference is smaller than the difference my own assumption
introduces, then the comparison is measuring me and not the structure. Moving the
modifiers ±10% is what separates the two.
</CriterionBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="method" label="Method">

<Method>

<Step n="01" title="Reference model, verified by hand">
A two-storey 10 × 10 m reinforced concrete building modelled in SAP2000. Bending and
shear checked by hand against Eurocode 2 before the model was used for anything, so
that the RC baseline was known to be right rather than assumed to be.
</Step>

<Step n="02" title="Conversion to steel–concrete">
The slab converted to an equivalent orthotropic representation — uniform thickness
and mass, with directional stiffness modifiers calculated per AISC N690-24 and
SCI P414, and calibrated against reference SteelBrick behaviour.
</Step>

<Step n="03" title="Sensitivity check">
Stiffness modifiers moved ±10% and the comparison re-run, to establish which
differences were the system and which were the modelling.
</Step>

<Step n="04" title="Aircraft impact assessment">
Penetration and scabbing evaluated under an aircraft impact scenario against
DOE-STD-3014 — the load case that decides whether a slab type is admissible at all
in this sector, regardless of how it performs statically.
</Step>

</Method>

</Section>

<Section id="means" label="What it means">

<div class="prose">

The sensitivity check is the same instinct as the second-pass interview analysis in
[Patchwork](../02-patchwork/) and the marking scheme in
[the signage study](../01-signage/): before believing a result, establish whether it
is the thing you measured or the way you measured it.

It is also the reason this project sits in a design portfolio. A modular system does
not get adopted in a regulated sector because it is faster. It gets adopted when
someone builds a case that a reviewer can check — which is a design problem about
evidence, made of structural engineering.
