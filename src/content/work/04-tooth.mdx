<Section id="question" label="The question">

<Lede text={frontmatter.question} />

Enamel is the only tissue in the human body that cannot grow back once it is lost, which
means that whatever happens to it simply stays happened.

That makes a tooth a record of two things at once: the person it belonged to, and the
world they were living in.

What came out of that is a single composite tooth, built from six European datasets and
playing from the start of the common era through to 1900.

</Section>

<div class="prose">

The piece opens with a jaw engraving inking itself in and swinging open, after which a
first molar and a canine turn to face the reader and a plane cuts them both open.

Then every kind of record arrives on them in time order: decay, wear, childhood stress
lines, lead in the enamel, and disease DNA rising from the root into the pulp.

One decision carries that whole figure, which is that distance from the centre stands
for how long ago a year is, on a square-root scale shared by every line, so that
seventeen centuries of pathogen data and six thousand years of metals can sit in one
picture without either of them vanishing.

Everything after the opening is set like a scientific journal, with running heads,
numbered plates and figures and notes rather than captions, because the piece asks to be
read as evidence and so it is laid out as evidence.

</div>

</Section>

<Section id="checking" label="Checking every number">

<div class="prose">

We used AI to pull figures out of papers, where the useful question was never whether to
use it but what had to be true before anything it produced could go on screen.

The answer we settled on was simple enough, which is that if a number was not in a paper
then it does not appear on screen.

<Section id="limits" label="What the data cannot say">

<div class="prose">

Nobody excavated Europe at random, and every site in the osteology module exists because
somebody had a reason to dig there, whether that reason was a grant, a motorway or a
cathedral restoration.

Three of its seventeen countries hold 48% of the individuals, Lithuania alone outweighs
Spain, Portugal, Greece, Hungary and Romania combined, and six European countries are not
in it at all.

The sources also disagree about when an era began and ended, so the periods overlap and
leave holes.

The gap that mattered most to the story got a slide of its own, because the industrial
tail is the thinnest part of every layer, resting on 796 adults across 7 sites and 4
countries and on only 10 human pathogen samples.

The narrative, in other words, peaks at precisely the point where the evidence beneath
it runs out.

The tooth on screen is therefore an abstraction rather than a reconstruction, drawn to
carry trends and relationships, and the page says where it is uncertain rather than
smoothing that uncertainty over.

</div>

</Section>

<Section id="chosen" label="The record we choose">

<div class="prose">

Everything above happened to people, whereas intervention is the first part of the
dental record that is deliberately chosen, being scheduled, paid for, consented to and
usually permanent.

A filling, a crown or a veneer will each overwrite the evidence underneath it.

That inverts the project, because modern teeth may say less about what the world did to
a person and more about what they chose to change, which makes them a record of access
and income as much as one of diet and disease.

</div>

<Section id="reflections" label="Reflections">

<div class="prose">

The rule I would carry into any project is that nothing goes on screen unless it traces
back to a row in a paper, which sounds at first like a research rule.

It is really a design rule, however, because it decides what the picture is allowed to
say, and I have now written one twice: once as a marking scheme for how much of a bus sign a passenger
can recall, once as a provenance check on what a model pulled out of a journal.

Working with AI on research made the gap between fast and finished very wide, because
getting a number out of a paper took seconds.

Establishing that it was the right number took the afternoon, and the afternoon is the
part that made the piece defensible.

The hardest thing to accept was putting our thinnest evidence at the loudest moment of
the story, since the industrial period is both where the argument lands and where the
data almost runs out.

The honest move was to keep the peak and say so, rather than quietly choosing a different
ending.

</div>

</Section>
