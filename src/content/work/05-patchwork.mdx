<Section id="question">

<Lede text={frontmatter.question} />

Patchwork is a slow social platform for sharing small cultural moments with people you
already know. I led the research and the framing on it. Partway through I stopped
writing things down and built a rough version instead, so the team had something to
click on rather than another document to read.

<Visit href="https://patchworkz.lovable.app/" label="Open the draft MVP" />

</Section>

<Section id="research">

I started with seven platforms, and compared them on five features that change how
people behave rather than on the values each one puts in its copy. What I was after was
a gap I could actually believe in, and the one I found is that none of them lets you
share something slow and personal without a public score attached to it.

Two users came out of the research, both 20 to 35. The first wants to share the small
but certain happiness of an ordinary day, ‘소확행’ in Korean, and has largely stopped,
because on the apps they already have those moments get skimmed past or taken the wrong
way. The second just wants a quieter way to stay close to a few friends, away from a
feed that is too fast and too polished to feel like anyone is really there.

<Section id="interviews">

The first round of interviews gave me roughly what I expected. People are tired of the
platforms they use, and the same themes kept coming back about connection and sharing
habits. All of it was true, and I nearly started building on it. What stopped me was
noticing that every one of those findings described how people behave on social media as
it already is, and that following them was quietly steering Patchwork towards being one
more of those apps.

So I went back through the same interviews a second time, reading them against what we
had already said the project was for. This time I was looking for the needs that were
not being met and the tensions sitting underneath the habits, rather than for behaviour
to copy. That second pass is what convinced me to put the idea in front of people as a
rough build before writing any UX strategy or wireframes.

What I learnt is that interview findings are not requirements. People told me what they
already do, and the design decision was in working out which parts of that to serve and
which parts were the problem I had set out to solve in the first place.

</Section>

<Section id="outcome">

Reading it that way narrowed the scope to things that support using the app on purpose:
light ways to save something and come back to it, structure around posting that you can
take or leave, and limits that make passive scrolling less rewarding.

Lovable made it cheap enough to build the thing rather than describe it, so I did, and
the MVP became what the team argued over instead of a feature list. Getting something
clickable in front of people first, then going back to UX strategy and wireframes with
what I had learnt from it, turned out to suit this project better than doing it the
other way round.

</Section>
