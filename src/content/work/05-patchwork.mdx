<Section id="question">

<Lede text={frontmatter.question} />

Patchwork is a slow social platform for sharing small cultural moments with people you
already know. I ran the research and the framing, then built a draft MVP so that the
team had something working to react to rather than a document.

<Visit href="https://patchworkz.lovable.app/" label="Open the draft MVP" />

</Section>

<Section id="research">

I compared seven platforms on five features that shape how people actually use them,
rather than on the values each one states. Nothing in the set offered slow, intimate,
culturally meaningful sharing without public validation attached to it. That absence is
the opening Patchwork is built into.

Two users came out of it, both 20 to 35. The primary one wants to share the small but
certain happiness of an ordinary day, ‘소확행’ in Korean, and has stopped posting because
on existing platforms those moments get consumed quickly or misread. The secondary one
wants a calmer way to stay close to a few friends, away from a feed that is too fast and
too polished.

<Section id="interviews">

The first round surfaced what you would expect: fatigue with existing platforms, and
recurring themes around connection and sharing habits. The findings were real, but they
described how people use social media now, and following them was quietly pulling
Patchwork towards being another one of those platforms.

So I read the same data a second time, against what the project had already said it was
for. That pass looked for unmet needs and tensions inside current behaviour instead of
for behaviour to reproduce, and it is what decided the project should be tested as a
rough MVP before any UX strategy or wireframes were written.

The lesson worth keeping is that interview findings are not requirements. The distance
between the two is where the design decision actually sits.

</Section>

<Section id="outcome">

The second analysis narrowed the scope to features that support intentional use:
lightweight ways to save and revisit content, optional structure around posting, and
limits that discourage passive scrolling.

I built the draft MVP myself to see those ideas working in context and used it as the
shared reference for deciding which of them to keep. UX strategy and wireframes come
after that rather than before it.

</Section>
